Bayes' Theorem

Bayes' theorem tells you how to update a belief when you see new evidence. Drag the sliders below and watch how the same evidence can mean very different things depending on what you believed beforehand.

2. Picture it as 1,000 people

3. Picture it as areas

Width = how common H is. Height of the shaded part = how likely the evidence is in that group. Posterior = blue area ÷ (blue + orange).

4. The theorem, with your numbers

P(H | E) = P(E | H) · P(H)P(E | H) · P(H) + P(E | ¬H) · P(¬H)

    In words: of all the ways the evidence could show up (true hits + false alarms), what fraction are true hits? When the hypothesis is rare, false alarms from the huge "not H" group can swamp the true hits, even with an accurate test. This is the base-rate fallacy.
