Bayes' theorem

Bayes' theorem tells you how to update a belief when you see new evidence. Start with how likely a hypothesis H was before (the prior), see how well the evidence E fits each possibility, and out comes the posterior: how likely H is now that you've seen E.

1. A crowd of 1,000 cases

Each dot is one case out of 1,000. Evidence-positive cases (E) are grouped at the top. Bayes' theorem asks: of the dots in that group, what share are blue? Hover a dot for details.

2. The same thing as areas

Width is the prior: the left column is H, the right is not-H. Within each column, height is how often E happens. Multiplying width × height gives each rectangle's area, a joint probability. The posterior is the blue area as a fraction of all the top (E) area.

What to try

Drag the prior down while keeping a very accurate test: the posterior collapses, because false alarms from the huge not-H group swamp the few true hits. This is the base-rate fallacy. Then set the false-alarm rate equal to the hit rate: the evidence says nothing, and the posterior equals the prior.
