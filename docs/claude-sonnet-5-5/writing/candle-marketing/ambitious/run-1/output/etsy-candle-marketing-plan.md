## 3. How to read your numbers

Your "uneven sales" may be partly just chance. At 37 orders a month, ordinary random variation alone gives a 95% range of **about 25 to 49 orders** (a standard deviation of about 6). A month of 28 followed by one of 46 can happen with nothing changing. Weekly orders swing even more: at about 9 a week, anything from 3 to 14 is normal.

That has three consequences for how you judge tactics:

1. **Judge on high-count numbers first.** Visits (about 1,850 a month, with a spread of only about 2%) and ad clicks are far more reliable than orders.
2. **Don't judge a tactic on one month of orders.** A genuine 25% lift in orders is only about 1 standard deviation above noise after one month, and reaches about 2 after four months.
3. **Conversion changes can't be proven at your size.** Telling 2.0% from 2.4% needs about 21,000 visits per version. Instead, judge listing changes by whether views and clicks rise over 8 or more weeks, and by what buyers say in reviews and messages.

(These figures assume orders arrive at random. Real shops are lumpier, so the true spread is at least this large.)

### Who buys, and why they'd choose you

- **Gift buyers** drive most of your November–December and February–May demand. They need three things: that it will arrive on time, that it looks gift-ready, and that it suits the recipient. That is why the plan stresses dated shipping promises, gift-ready photos and recipient-led titles.
- **Self-care and home buyers** care about scent notes, how the candle burns and how it looks on a shelf. Scent descriptions, lifestyle photos and reviews that mention burn quality serve them.
- **Your angle.** Against larger shops, a buyer picks you for one clear reason. Write it as a single sentence: "Small-batch soy candles for [who/what theme], hand-poured in [place], gift-ready." You will write it in Week 7, once the competitor worksheet shows what the others already claim. It then goes into your About section, the first line of each description and your packaging.

### The listing rebuild

- **Title:** put the phrase a gift buyer would type first, then the scent and size. For example: `Lavender Soy Candle Gift for Her, Hand Poured 8oz Jar, Spa Gift Candle`. Use most of the 140 characters, but make it read naturally.
- **13 tags, all used:** roughly 4 gift or occasion (birthday gift for her, housewarming gift), 4 scent or mood, 3 material or handmade, 2 style or room.
- **Photos, in this order:** (1) clean first image that reads at thumbnail size, (2) lit candle in a styled room, (3) scale shot, (4) label and wick close-up, (5) burn time and ingredients graphic, (6) gift-ready packaging, (7–10) scent variants and packing. Add a 5–15 second video: pouring, lighting or unboxing.
- **Description:** keywords and the hook in the first 160 characters, then wax, wick, burn time, size and care.
- **Claims:** describe scent and mood, but don't promise health benefits (stress relief, better sleep). Don't call the candle "non-toxic", "natural" or "eco-friendly" unless you can document it; the FTC treats these as claims you must be able to back up. Publish a burn time only after you have tested it.
- **One main search phrase per listing, never shared.** Two of your listings built around the same phrase compete with each other. Pick 5 phrases you most want to win (for example "soy candle gift for her") and give each to one listing as its lead phrase. Each week, search all 5 in a private browser window and record whether a listing of yours is on page 1. This is a direct reading of rank that doesn't depend on your small order counts.
- **Sell by recipient, not by scent.** Gift buyers search for who the gift is for. Frame your three price points that way (table below), and show the set next to the jar on the same listing page so the jar looks like the easy middle choice.
- **Method:** top 3 listings in Week 1, then 5 a week in January until all 25 are done.

| Price point | Recipient framing | Typical item |

### Order value and the shipping threshold

- Etsy launched its free-shipping guarantee in 2019: shops that ship free on US orders of **$35 or more** get priority in US search ([Etsy investor release](https://investors.etsy.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/117/etsy-unveils-plans-to-make-free-shipping-a-core-part-of-the-shopping-experience)). I could not confirm that this is still how search works today, so **verify the current rule in Shop Manager and treat the guarantee as a decision, not a default.** Your average order of about $32 sits just under $35.
- **The catch is profit.** Free shipping turns postage from a cost the buyer pays into a cost you pay. The margin check below shows that a bundle built to cross the threshold can earn *less* per order than a plain jar.
- For a rough benchmark, roundups report a $25 single soy candle and a $55 boxed set of four travel candles as customer favorites ([Apartment Therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/candles-on-etsy-36653171)). That is secondhand reporting, so treat it as a rough benchmark.

### Margin check: does the order-value lever raise profit?

Revenue targets say nothing about what you keep. Etsy fees are about 6.5% transaction plus 3% + $0.25 payment processing, plus a $0.20 listing fee per sale (secondary sources: [Checkout Page](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees)). I use 9.5% of the order plus $0.45. Offsite Ads, when an order is attributed to one, take a further 12% of the order (15% if your shop sold under $10,000 in the past 365 days). The last column uses 12%, because at $1,200 a month you are probably already over that line.

Illustrative figures, **all my estimates**: an 8 oz jar costs you $9 in materials, a mini $3.50, and postage runs $8–12 per package (inferred from published Ground Advantage prices; your Etsy label screen shows the real cost). Replace these with your own.

| Case | Order | Etsy fees | Materials | Postage you pay | Profit | Profit if Offsite Ad (12%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. 1 jar $28 + $7 shipping paid by buyer | $35 | $3.78 | $9.00 | $9.00 | $13.23 | $9.03 |
| B. Jar + mini add-on, $39, free shipping | $39 | $4.16 | $12.50 | $9.50 | $12.85 | $8.17 |
| C. 2-jar set, $52, free shipping | $52 | $5.39 | $18.00 | $12.00 | $16.61 | $10.37 |
| D. 3-mini set, $38, free shipping | $38 | $4.06 | $10.50 | $8.00 | $15.44 | $10.88 |
| E. 2-jar set, $38, free shipping | $38 | $4.06 | $18.00 | $12.00 | $3.94 | $-0.62 |

What this shows:
- **A $38 two-jar set (E) is the trap.** It clears the threshold and earns about $4. A two-jar set needs a price of about **$47 or more** to reach $12 profit with free shipping.
- **Light sets (D) and add-on minis (B) are the better order-value levers.** They add revenue without adding much postage.
- **Offsite Ads cut profit per order by about $4–$6 on attributed orders at the 12% rate (more at 15%).** Case E goes negative.
- **Discounts eat the margin too.** After the 10% Black Friday discount, case B earns $9.32, case C $11.90 and case D $12.00. So the rule for a sale is a profit of at least $9 per order after the discount.
- **Rule for every bundle you build: at least $12 profit per order at full price** by your own materials and postage numbers, checked before listing. Case B only passes at $39, not $38.
- At about $12–16 profit per order, 75 orders a month in spring is roughly **$960–$1,250 gross profit, before your $150 marketing spend.** The same math at the baseline (37 orders at about $13) is about $490.

### Etsy Ads

Etsy Ads uses **one daily budget for the whole shop**, with a $1 minimum ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/articles/360033701174)). You choose which listings to promote and Etsy spreads the budget between them, so you can't set a separate amount per listing. Start by promoting your top 2 listings by conversion. In November–December the budget is $80 a month (about $2.65 a day). In January–April it is $60 (about $2 a day). Etsy's seller handbook suggests $3–5 a day for a first test, so ours is a lean test and its results will be noisy (Section 3).

**Offsite Ads are separate from Etsy Ads, and may already apply to you.** Etsy charges 15% of an attributed order if your shop sold under $10,000 in the past 365 days (optional), or 12% if it sold $10,000 or more (mandatory). Etsy checks on the first day of each month, and once you cross $10,000 the 12% rate stays permanently ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-in/articles/360000338367-How-Etsy-s-Offsite-Ads-Work), [Etsy fees](https://www.etsy.com/au/help/article/4530)). If you have averaged $1,200 a month for a year, you are probably already over $10,000. Check your Shop Manager and include the fee in your pricing.

### Reviews and repeat buyers

A candle is used up, so a happy buyer is a natural repeat customer. Four small pieces cover it:
- **In the package:** a thank-you note that invites an honest review, a care card with burn-safety and care tips, and a signup card with a QR code. Keep the return-buyer code on its own card.
- **Welcome email** when someone signs up: your story, bestsellers and care tips.
- **Replenish reminder** 6–8 weeks after signup, since a regularly burned 8 oz jar runs out in about that time (my estimate; adjust to your tested burn time).
- **Seasonal announcements** to the list, a few a quarter, never more than one a week.

### Can you make it? Production calendar

This section is about making candles, not marketing, so none of it counts toward your 5 hours. A plan that sells candles you can't make or ship in time hurts you more than a quiet week does. Soy candles are usually left to cure for about 1–2 weeks before they burn and smell at their best (supplier guidance, such as [BrambleBerry](https://www.brambleberry.com/faqs/how-long-does-it-take-for-candle-wax-to-dry.html); test your own wax and fragrance). I use the conservative end, 14 days.

| Period | Orders | Candles per order | Candles | With 20% buffer | Pour by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| December | 55 | 1.4 | 77 | 92 | Nov 16 (first batch), Nov 30 (second batch) |
| Steady spring month | 75 | 1.4 | 105 | 126 | Pour about 29 candles a week |

- **Make-to-stock for your top 3 scents, make-to-order for the rest.** Pour the top 3 now (Nov 14–16) so they are cured by Cyber Monday. Check your sales by scent first; I am guessing the top 3 carry most of your orders.
- **Pour-by dates:** December gifts Nov 30 (for the Dec 14 last-order date), Valentine's Jan 25 (for a Feb 8 ship-by), Mother's Day Apr 16 (for an Apr 30 ship-by).
- **Processing time:** the "ships in" time on a listing must include any cure time for scents you don't hold in stock.
- **Candles per order (1.4) and the 20% buffer are my estimates.** Use your real figures once you have them.

### Keeping it going: the minimum viable week

The likeliest way this plan fails is a busy week at your day job followed by giving up. So it has a built-in short version:

- **Three fixed slots, in your calendar tonight.** For example Tuesday evening (1.5 hours), Thursday evening (1.5 hours) and Saturday morning (2 hours). A slot you have to find time for each week gets skipped.
- **The first row of each week's table is the must-do, and the scorecard is the second.** That is the minimum viable week, never more than 2.5 hours. If a week goes wrong, do those two and stop. Nothing else in that week is lost for good, because the cycle repeats.
- **If-then rule:** if you miss a slot, drop the lowest row of that week, not the scorecard. Don't try to catch up by doubling next week.

## 8. Kill-or-keep rules

These thresholds are my starting points, not Etsy figures. Adjust them once you have real data. Check each rule on the review date in the weekly plan.

| Tactic | Judge by | Keep if | Cut or change if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Etsy Ads, early reading (per listing) | Clicks and cost per click, at 4 weeks | Cost per click is $0.80 or less | Cost per click is above $0.80 after 100 clicks, or there are fewer than 100 clicks after 4 weeks (the listing is barely shown) |
| Etsy Ads, return | Return on ad spend (revenue ÷ spend), only once there are 5+ attributed orders | 3× or better | Below 2×. Hold between 2× and 3× for 4 more weeks. If neither test can be read by Week 11 (about $150 spent), pause ads and move the money to creator sets. |
| Sales and discounts | Profit per order after the discount, and visits during the sale. Don't judge on order lift alone: holiday seasonality flatters every sale. | Profit per order stays at $9 or more after the discount and visits rise | Otherwise stop discounting and use bundles instead |
| Instagram/Pinterest content | Visits from social in Etsy traffic sources, over 8 weeks | Social is 8% or more of visits, or has produced 3+ orders | Cut back to Pinterest pins only, 20 minutes a week |
| Creator gifting | Orders from the batch, and posts that carry the gift disclosure | At least 1 order or 1 compliant post per 2 sets sent | Stop after 2 batches with neither |
| Listing rewrites | Views for that listing, 4 weeks before and after | Views up 15% or more | Revert the title and look at the photo |
| Gift sets and bundles | Profit per order first, then average order value | $12 or more profit per order and average order value of $36 or more over 8 weeks | Reprice, lighten the set, or drop it |
| Price rises | Views and profit per order after 4 weeks (orders are too noisy to read) | Views hold within 15% and profit per order rises | Revert |
| Email | Signups per month | 10 or more a month by Month 3 | Move the signup card to a more prominent spot, don't add more emails |
